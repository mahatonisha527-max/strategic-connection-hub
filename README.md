# Strategic Connections Hub

2. BUILD RAVECLUE.COM — STANDALONE BUSINESS WEBSITE

1. Project Context

You are creating a new standalone website for:

Domain: raveclue.com
Website/brand name: Raveclue
Primary business reference: orlint.com

Use the provided ORLINT_WEBSITE_ANALYSIS.md as the factual foundation for understanding the underlying business, its capabilities, audiences, terminology, tone, and design quality. The source material is the authority for factual claims. Do not silently invent information that is not supported.

2. Core Positioning

Position Raveclue as strategic business connections. The site should communicate strategic connections, relationship building, relevant introductions, long-term fit. Use this description as the strategic direction, not as permission to invent operational details.

Tone: professional, discreet, intelligent, confident, concise, human, and relationship-oriented. Do not make the brand sound like a mass lead-generation operation, generic SaaS product, marketplace, or high-volume outreach service.

3. Relationship to Orlint

This is a separate domain with its own website. It may be professionally related to the broader Orlint ecosystem, but it must not pretend to be an unrelated company and must not attempt to hide a legitimate relationship. If the relationship is mentioned, do so naturally and transparently. Keep the secondary brand visually and editorially independent.

Do NOT repeatedly use 'by Orlint' as the main identity. Do not make Orlint the dominant visual brand. Do not create a misleading impression of independence if a relationship is disclosed.

4. Website Objective

When someone searches for or visits raveclue.com, they should understand within a few seconds: what Raveclue does, who it is for, what kind of business needs or opportunities it addresses, why its approach is deliberate rather than high-volume, and how to start a conversation.

Primary conversion: a conversation/contact request, not a hard-sell purchase.

5. Recommended Site Structure

Create a polished small-business website with these pages:

Home

About

Services

Contact

Privacy

Add an FAQ only if it genuinely improves the user experience. Do not create thin SEO pages.

6. Homepage — Information Architecture

Hero — Create a distinctive hero around strategic connections, relationship building, relevant introductions, long-term fit. Write an original headline rather than copying Orlint or another domain. Include a concise explanation, one primary CTA such as 'Start a Conversation', and a restrained visual element.

The Business Need — Explain the practical friction organizations face when searching for relevant partners, providers, capabilities, or opportunities. Avoid unsupported numerical claims.

What the Brand Does — Present 3–4 capabilities consistent with the source analysis. Use only capabilities that are genuinely supported, such as business introductions, capability discovery, opportunity matching, or partnership connections. Tailor the emphasis to this domain.

How It Works — Use a simple 4-step flow: share the need or opportunity → consider the fit → make the introduction → the parties take the relationship forward.

Who It Helps — Choose only the most relevant audience groups from the source material. Do not claim to serve every possible audience unless the content supports it.

Why This Approach — Build 3–4 differentiators around relevance over volume, deliberate introductions, relationship-led development, and long-term thinking. Present these as philosophy, not verified performance statistics.

Editorial Brand Statement — Create an original, visually strong statement that captures this domain's philosophy. Do not reuse Orlint's signature phrases.

Final CTA — End with an understated invitation to discuss a business need, capability, opportunity, or potential connection.

7. About Page

Explain what Raveclue is, what it focuses on, its approach to business relationships, why relevance matters, and how introductions or discovery are approached. Do not invent founding dates, employees, offices, awards, clients, revenue, certifications, or company history. If Orlint is referenced, disclose the relationship naturally.

8. Services Page

Create a focused services page with 3–5 capabilities. Each service should have a clear name, concise explanation, appropriate audience/use case, value proposition, and CTA. Avoid generic buzzword lists.

9. Contact Page

Use a premium, minimal contact experience. Suggested fields: Name, Work email, Company, What are you looking for?, and Message. Do not claim that a form sends anything unless a real backend/integration exists. If no backend is configured, make the integration requirement explicit rather than pretending.

10. Privacy Page

Create a proper privacy-page structure. Do not invent legal-entity information, jurisdictional claims, or legal assurances. If approved legal text is unavailable, clearly mark the content as placeholder copy requiring replacement.

11. Visual Direction

Create a distinct Raveclue identity while inheriting the Orlint quality bar: editorial, minimalist, premium, warm-neutral, restrained, typography-led, and relationship-oriented.

Visual personality: quietly premium, modern, established, editorial, human, trustworthy, intentional. Avoid generic SaaS layouts, excessive gradients, glassmorphism, excessive rounded cards, neon colors, cliché handshake photography, random office stock imagery, excessive animation, and generic AI illustrations.

Use typography as a major design element. Cormorant Garamond and Inter may be used as inspiration, but do not simply clone Orlint's exact typography system. Develop a restrained palette compatible with the broader brand philosophy while giving this domain a slightly differentiated visual character.

12. Layout & Interaction

Use generous whitespace, strong editorial hierarchy, thin rules, restrained cards, large typography, clear CTA hierarchy, and occasional asymmetrical compositions. Do not make every section a card grid. Use subtle reveal/hover/button transitions only, respect prefers-reduced-motion, and ensure the experience is excellent on desktop, tablet, and mobile.

13. Accessibility

Use semantic HTML, logical heading hierarchy, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, accessible forms, descriptive labels, appropriate contrast, useful alt text, reduced-motion support, and accessible mobile navigation.

14. SEO

Configure unique page titles, unique meta descriptions, absolute canonical URLs, Open Graph metadata, Twitter/X metadata, favicon, robots.txt, sitemap, and semantic HTML. Canonical domain: https://raveclue.com. Do not keyword-stuff, create doorway pages, or create SEO spam.

15. Critical Infrastructure Rule — No Redirect

Raveclue must be a real standalone website. A visitor entering https://raveclue.com must remain on raveclue.com and receive the actual website.

Do NOT redirect this domain to orlint.com.

Do NOT iframe orlint.com.

Do NOT reverse-proxy or mask orlint.com.

Do NOT clone the Orlint website.

Do NOT automatically forward visitors to another domain.

16. Keep It Distinct

Do not copy Orlint's hero copy, signature phrases, exact section sequence, exact service descriptions, exact navigation, exact footer, exact CTA wording, exact visual composition, or exact card layouts. Use the Orlint analysis to understand the business and quality standard, not as a template to duplicate.

17. Technical Implementation

Use a modern, maintainable frontend architecture. Prefer the existing Orlint stack when appropriate: React, TypeScript, Vite, TanStack Start, and Tailwind CSS. Keep the site lightweight, use reusable components where useful, and avoid unnecessary dependencies.

18. Final Quality Checklist

No fabricated claims, testimonials, clients, statistics, certifications, or history.

No copied Orlint marketing copy.

All navigation and CTAs work.

Forms are honest about backend/integration status.

No console errors or build errors.

No broken routes or horizontal overflow.

Responsive across breakpoints.

Unique titles/descriptions and correct canonical URL.

Sitemap and robots.txt are present.

Keyboard navigation, focus states, labels, headings, and reduced-motion support work.

https://raveclue.com is genuinely standalone and does not redirect to Orlint.

19. Final Creative Direction

Do not make Raveclue look like 'Orlint.com with a different name.' Make it look like a focused, sophisticated raveclue business presence with its own identity, built from the same underlying philosophy of deliberate relationships and relevant opportunities.

Build the complete website now. Before finalizing, verify that every factual statement is supported by the provided source material or is clearly framed as positioning/philosophy rather than a factual claim.
